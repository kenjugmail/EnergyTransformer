# REWT repairs: implementation complete, quality gains unproven

The proposed repairs are implemented and the controlled follow-up is complete. Centered output coordinates remove the arbitrary common-logit energy direction, and local refinement now slightly improves validation prediction loss in all three revised Fourier checkpoints. The resulting model has **no clear quality advantage over the raw or non-Fourier energy controls**. Generated reasoning remains near zero, every sampled text continuation received the lowest coherence score in the exploratory assistant review, and the full model still has severe optimization spikes despite completing with finite updates.

The [implementation record](/home/kt/Documents/EnergyTransformer/reports/rewt_stabilized_20261009/IMPLEMENTATION.md) and [package commands](/home/kt/Documents/EnergyTransformer/rewt/README.md) describe the changes: consistent centered geometry, noisy initial guesses, hard wrong-byte comparisons, refinement-repair feedback, variable training depth, a balanced reasoning curriculum with first-error labels, configurable Fourier capacity, causal history caching, bounded optional inference stopping, and residual gradient filters that preserve the raw gradient. Learned weights remain fixed at inference; accepted refinements decrease the same complete conditional joint energy. Fourteen short validation screens selected 32 modes, frequency bound 2, step size 0.25, learning rate 0.004 and ordinary clipping. Residual filtering performed better than replacement filtering in that screen but did not beat the selected clipping recipe.

Each language fit starts from project-trained random weights and processes 1,048,576 target bytes, approximately 17.1 times the original pilot's budget. The primary comparison uses five approximately 250,000-parameter variants and three seeds. Reasoning copies receive 800 updates of batch 16. The [full numerical report](/home/kt/Documents/EnergyTransformer/reports/rewt_stabilized_20261009/REPORT.md) includes raw outputs, teacher preparation costs, inference policies and parameter counts. Values below are means ± sample standard deviations across seeds; these are not confidence intervals. Lower bits per byte (bpb) is better. Complete reasoning requires every intermediate step and the final answer on arithmetic and rule-chain tasks; recall is separate.

Revised Fourier minus raw test bpb is +0.01648, −0.03215 and +0.00896 across the paired seeds: a mean improvement of only 0.00223 bpb, with two seeds worsening. Revised Fourier minus revised neural energy averages **+0.00170 bpb**, slightly worse. Complete reasoning has no mean improvement over raw REWT. All five primary arms score 0% complete correctness on the withheld-wording/four-step reasoning subset. A training-derived constant response with oracle family information reaches 1% complete reasoning. These results do not demonstrate useful reasoning.

The original Transformer allocation minimizes parameter-count error and has width 48 with a large feedforward block. A separate [baseline check](/home/kt/Documents/EnergyTransformer/reports/rewt_stabilized_20261009/baseline_supplement/REPORT.md) screened widths 48/80/96 and three learning rates on validation, then trained the selected width-80, 250,122-parameter Transformer across three seeds. It reaches **3.980 ± 0.019 test bpb**, **4.030 ± 0.037 bpb at the equal CPU budget**, 3.7 ± 0.6% exact reasoning answers, 1.5 ± 0.5% complete reasoning and 58.0 ± 1.7% recall. Primary revised Fourier recall is 10.3 ± 4.9%. REWT's lower bpb at equal language bytes therefore coexists with higher training cost, poor generation and weak recall. This short supplemental screen does not establish an optimally tuned Transformer, and it does not change the full model's original teacher.

The [local diagnostics](/home/kt/Documents/EnergyTransformer/reports/rewt_stabilized_20261009/DIAGNOSTICS.md) confirm the geometry repair. The raw control devotes 58.54% of its initial output gradient's squared norm to moving every logit together. The revised models' corresponding share is numerically zero. Adding one to every logit changes raw energy by about 102.24 while leaving its probabilities unchanged; revised energy changes only at numerical rounding scale. Revised Fourier's fixed-prefix two-round bpb changes are −0.001739, −0.000221 and −0.000404, a mean of −0.000788. Eight rounds worsen one seed. These small local improvements are separate from autoregressive generation, which changes subsequent conditioning.

Energy remains an imperfect prediction signal. The revised Fourier energy-gradient/CE-gradient cosine averages approximately −0.0036, and its learned wave output-gradient norm averages 11.39% of the quadratic-term norm. In the [candidate probe](/home/kt/Documents/EnergyTransformer/reports/rewt_stabilized_20261009/ENERGY_SIGNAL.md), the true byte has lower energy than the strongest wrong byte in 47.14% of positions, or 21.38% where the current prediction is wrong. Those fixed-latent, one-hot comparisons are diagnostics, not accuracy estimates for a language distribution. Lower energy and an early stopping threshold still do not certify a correct answer.

The first follow-up stopped during full-model reasoning. An exact saved-optimizer replay found a finite gradient with a true norm about 5.20 × 10²⁰ whose float32 norm overflowed. Float64 norm/power arithmetic repairs that error; a longer replay then exposed actual non-finite recurrent gradients. All three replacement full reasoning fits restart from their completed language weights and bound training credit through carried latent states to sixteen observed tokens. Forward state values and observed history are retained, and inference still uses the full 128-byte window. The other twenty-seven completed fits reuse byte-identical parent checkpoints and original training/source records; archived/current inference logits pass the recorded bitwise parity probe.

The [long-run stability audit](/home/kt/Documents/EnergyTransformer/reports/rewt_stabilized_20261009/STABILITY.md) is a material qualification. All restarted objectives and gradient components stay finite and applied gradients remain clipped near norm one, but full-model raw norms still peak at **1.49 × 10¹⁷, 1.22 × 10²⁰ and 2.14 × 10²⁰**. Clipping affects 35.1%, 68.9% and 42.4% of updates; maximum total losses are 25.9, 99.8 and 284. Completing training does not mean the full model learns smoothly. It has not earned a scale-up recommendation.

All **450 anonymous primary text continuations** were actually read and scored before the model/seed key was opened; ratings and sample hashes were locked first. Every continuation received coherence 1/5. Raw and revised Fourier mean fluency are both 1.99/5, with occasional word fragments and broken syntax/markup. This [review](/home/kt/Documents/EnergyTransformer/reports/rewt_stabilized_20261009/TEXT_QUALITY_REVIEW.md) is an exploratory assistant assessment by the implementation author. Independent human ratings remain pending. All ninety supplemental Transformer continuations were also read and locked before joining seed identities, with the model family known; fluency and coherence are both 1/5. Neither review supplies independent human preference evidence.

Language training plus teacher preparation averages 48.9 CPU seconds for the primary Transformer, 230.2 for raw REWT, 329.5 for neural energy, 337.5 for revised Fourier and 1101.3 for full REWT. The fourteen primary screens cost an additional 1103.7 training CPU seconds and 7.2 validation CPU seconds; the nine supplemental screens are a separate tuning cost. The fresh equal-time fits above are independent random-weight fits, and full REWT charges its fresh teacher within the 60-second allowance. A final update can overshoot the cap.

Separate [actual generation profiles](/home/kt/Documents/EnergyTransformer/reports/rewt_stabilized_20261009/GENERATION_RUNTIME.md) use one CPU thread, fresh processes, a 32-byte prefix, 96 generated bytes, a warm-up and three timed repetitions. Single-sample CPU milliseconds per generated byte are 1.013 ± 0.070 for the primary Transformer, 0.952 ± 0.258 raw, 1.135 ± 0.618 neural, 0.893 ± 0.176 revised Fourier and 4.002 ± 1.518 full. Ten-sample rates are 1.026, 0.123, 0.143, 0.114 and 0.504 respectively. The supplemental Transformer measures 0.921 single-sample and 0.739 at ten samples. The Transformer implementation recomputes prefixes while REWT carries state and cached observed history. These are measured implementation costs on a shared CPU host, not evidence about optimal cached Transformers or GPU efficiency. Process RSS includes Python/Torch and is not a precise activation-memory peak.

Before training, **50 implementation tests passed**, covering analytic gradients, shift invariance, energy acceptance, fixed inference weights, causal execution, retained forward memory, training-only temporal boundaries, process labels, finite-gradient clipping, true non-finite rejection, filters, optimizer continuation and reuse guards. Final audits verify the frozen source and snapshots, unchanged reused checkpoint/training bytes, disjoint task identities, exclusion of both prior test sets, common language budgets and validation-only inference selection. There are 4,500 saved primary task generations, 450 primary text samples, 900 supplemental task generations and ninety supplemental text samples. The [verification record](/home/kt/Documents/EnergyTransformer/reports/rewt_stabilized_20261009/verification.json) distinguishes implementation checks, mechanical completion and pending human review.

The experiment is small: three seeds, about 250K parameters, 128-byte resets, 4,096 held-out language bytes and 200 arithmetic/rule cases per seed. The repairs are bundled, Fourier-mode comparisons redistribute feedforward capacity, screening is short and uses one seed, teachers are weak, and full-model temporal training credit is restricted. New test samples prevent partial results from the stopped run entering this evaluation, but also prevent paired numerical comparison with the original pilot. A useful next experiment would first stabilize the full training gradients and establish one-step reasoning and prediction-aligned refinement in the core model; adding modules or scale is not supported by these results. That recommendation is separate from the completed implementation.

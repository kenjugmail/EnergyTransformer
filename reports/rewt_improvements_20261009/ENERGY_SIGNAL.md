# Energy signal in the revised checkpoints

This read-only probe uses four declared validation windows, 512 byte positions per checkpoint, and all three seeds. Every checkpoint file's SHA256 was checked before and after the probe. No test example, weight update or selection change is involved. The reference prefix conditioning comes from the original two-round rollout, then remains fixed for candidate comparisons.

The nonlinear-gradient ratio is the mean per-position output-gradient norm from the learned nonlinear energy term divided by the quadratic-term norm. For Fourier models the nonlinear term is cosine; the neural control uses tanh. A ratio is not a share of total energy or squared gradient norm, and does not by itself measure usefulness.

| Variant | Nonlinear / quadratic output gradient % | True candidate beats fixed wrong % | True beats strongest wrong % | True beats strongest wrong when prediction is wrong % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Raw REWT control | 13.94 | 97.27 | 43.68 | 19.32 |
| Revised neural energy | 0.73 | 94.14 | 47.79 | 22.34 |
| Revised Fourier REWT | 11.39 | 93.95 | 47.14 | 21.38 |
| Revised full REWT | 9.33 | 93.49 | 44.66 | 24.19 |

The Fourier term still contributes a smaller output gradient than the quadratic term. Increasing the number of modes from 16 to 32 and normalizing the frequency blocks did not automatically make that contribution stronger. This observation does not prove that increasing its amplitude would improve quality; it would require a new validation study and energy-acceptance checks.

Candidates replace the output block with a two-unit one-hot vector for the true byte, a fixed incorrect byte, or the model's strongest incorrect byte, while retaining identical latent states and prefix conditioning. These comparisons mirror the ranking auxiliary loss. Language can have multiple plausible next bytes, so these percentages are not an accuracy or correctness certificate.

Revised basic energy models rank hard candidates somewhat better than the raw control in this probe, while the raw control ranks the easy fixed negative better. The full model is best on the conditional hard-ranking measure but falls behind the revised basic models on the overall hard-ranking measure. Training repairs, geometry, mode allocation and frequency scaling change together, so these differences cannot be assigned to a single fix.
